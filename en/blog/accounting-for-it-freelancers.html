What accounting an IT freelancer or developer needs

Published · Orbita Group · 6 min read

Developers and IT freelancers in Uzbekistan usually have the same first question: how to legally register income when clients are foreign companies and payment arrives in foreign currency.

Which registration format fits

For a small, stable income from one-off projects, self-employed status is often enough. If income grows, regular clients appear or an official contract with a legal entity is needed — consider a sole proprietorship. A separate option for teams and startups is registering as an IT Park resident, which provides tax benefits but requires more serious document flow.

What to do with foreign-currency income

Receipts from foreign clients are foreign-currency revenue, and it has to be recorded correctly in the books, not just moved to a card like an ordinary transfer. Mistakes here are one of the common reasons for questions during audits of freelancers whose income is growing.

Common mistakes

  • They don't declare income from foreign clients, assuming that since the money comes "from abroad" — it's a personal matter
  • They confuse self-employed status with ordinary employment and don't pay any taxes at all
  • They keep no records, even in a simple form, which makes it hard to prove that the income is legal when needed

Conclusion

The choice between self-employment, a sole proprietorship and IT Park depends on the volume and stability of income — you should revisit the format as you grow rather than staying on the first status you picked for years.
