What accounting a beauty salon or beauty professional needs

Published · Orbita Group · 5 min read

Between a specialist who works alone in a rented chair and a salon with a team of ten — the difference is not only scale, but also which accounting and tax format fits.

Which registration format fits

A solo specialist most often chooses a patent or self-employment — it is simpler for a one-off or irregular flow of clients. A salon with employees and permanent premises rental usually registers as a sole proprietorship or LLC with full HR and tax accounting.

What to keep in mind

Certain procedures (for example, some types of cosmetology with a medical angle) may require a license — this should be checked for each specific service before starting work. In the books it is important to record consumables (materials, tools) correctly and not to mix personal expenses with salon expenses — especially if the space is rented under an informal arrangement called "pay per chair".

Common mistakes

  • They mix personal and business expenses, which makes it impossible to understand real profitability
  • They work on a verbal chair rental without a contract or supporting documents
  • They don't check whether a specific procedure requires a license and risk facing an inspection

Conclusion

The more clearly personal and business money are separated from the very beginning, the easier it is for a salon to grow and hire new specialists without confusion in reporting. We'll help you choose the format and set up accounting for your salon or private practice.
